about brain.

You already think about thousands of things. Most of them disappear.

Your best ideas rarely arrive at the perfect moment. A question appears while you're walking. A useful article gets buried in a tab. A thought makes sense six months later. brain. gives those moments a place to stay, so time can work for you instead of erasing them.

01

Keep the spark.

Drop in thoughts, ideas, links, notes, study material, or anything you want to remember.

02

Return without digging.

Find things again through memory, search, dates, and collections — without building a filing system first.

03

Notice your own patterns.

Over time, old things become context. You start seeing what keeps coming back, changing, and connecting.

the long game

The longer you use it, the more it becomes yours.

At first, brain. is simply a place to put things. Months later, it becomes a record of what interested you, what you were learning, what you wanted to make, and what you kept returning to.

brain. is a personal memory space for the things you notice, think about, learn, want to make, or return to later.

Anything worth keeping: a thought, idea, note, study point, useful link, question, or something you know future-you may want back.

No. The point is to capture first. brain. should reduce the need to build a filing system before you can start using it.

Notes are the starting point. The bigger idea is memory: finding things again, rediscovering old thoughts, and eventually seeing connections over time.

In this current prototype, your saved items stay in your browser. Cloud accounts, syncing, backups, and the deeper intelligence layer are planned for later.
